Fieldnote

November 2026 · Retired

I built Fieldnote after getting tired of writing a Markdown handoff every time a sports research chat grew too long. The app gave eight sports a permanent thread, brought forward the rules and past decisions for each one, and ran web research in the background. A shared table was supposed to make the latest recommendations and price conditions easy to find.

GitHub

What it did

  • Persistent context. Each sport kept its full conversation. Imported handoffs became drafts I could review, and the worker combined recent messages with relevant rules, history, and game state for each new request.
  • Background research. Requests went into Postgres and a separate worker handled the model call. I could leave the page and come back to check the result. The app saved the original response and validated the game, market, price, and sources before publishing structured rows.
  • A separate ledger. A recommendation or a quoted price could stay in the analysis. A confirmed entry required an explicit action, and settlement used the recorded terms and verified result. Missing terms stayed unknown.

What the operating record showed

Of 423 recorded requests, 18 ended in failure or interruption. That figure reflects final job states. It leaves out earlier attempts that recovered and completed requests that still had missing rows. A seven-sport audit made the distinction clear. All seven raw responses were saved, but each initially failed to produce a validated table. Two lacked an evaluation block, four failed schema validation, and one failed to match a cited URL to its source record.

Because the raw responses were available, I could reprocess them offline and recover the rows with adequate evidence. The experience made publication a separate milestone from model completion. A usable result needs to reach the table with its source, decision, and price condition intact.

Why it stopped

Fieldnote reduced the work of moving context between chats, but I still spent time retrying runs and reconciling the chat with the table. When a row was missing, I went back to the original analysis to see what the table had missed. The performance review also had a limit. The available Fieldnote and ChatGPT bet records covered different dates, markets, and accepted prices, so they could not establish which workflow performed better under the same conditions. I retired the app in November 2026.

What I would measure next time

I would track how often the first request produces a usable published result, how often rows are missing, and how long recovery takes. For a comparison of the analyses themselves, I would hold the pregame inputs and price snapshot fixed and keep recommended bets separate from confirmed entries and settlements.

Tech stack and closeout

Next.js and TypeScript for the app, Supabase Postgres for the queue and ledger, and a separate worker and gateway for model calls. I tested a database restore and verified the gateway backup before shutting down the Railway services and pausing Supabase.
