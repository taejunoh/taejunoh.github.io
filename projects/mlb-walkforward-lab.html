MLB Walk-Forward Lab

September 2026 · Retired

I built a walk-forward MLB forecasting experiment that saved timestamped pregame claims and traced each one through its source inputs and final result. The archive contains 24 claims across three regular-season dates. Of those, 23 formally settled: 10 were correct, or 43.5% of the settled claims. One claim remains unresolved.

Public archive

How I counted

Forecast coverage and formal settlement are different counts. The 24 timestamped pregame claims describe what the experiment recorded; the accuracy figure uses only the 23 claims with a formal settlement. The first eligible claim for each game was preserved as the immutable forecast record, then matched to the official final result. The source trace keeps the recorded forecast and its inputs inspectable rather than replacing them with a later version.

The 43.5% figure is a small descriptive result, not evidence of a betting edge or profit. It covers three regular-season dates only and does not support a broad claim about MLB forecasting performance.

What the data checks established

An isolated reconciliation of official data accounted for 2,469 played games and 728,343 pitches. Separately, the archived offline fixture run passed 33 tests and 9 subtests. These checks support the integrity of the saved inputs and processing path; they are not a live evaluation of postseason forecasts. No live postseason test was performed, and I make no postseason performance claim.

Why it stopped

On September 30, 2026, I chose to pause after input validation was blocked by a duplicate game ID associated with a postponed game and its rescheduled entry. A later parser fix and offline verification addressed the input issue, but that repair did not restart the experiment. I did not resume live forecasting or extend the record into the postseason.

Closeout

The English operations closeout documents the pause and verification. The public archive preserves the code and evidence.

The November archive preserves 380 original, self-generated evidence files—including results, receipts, manifests, acquisition metadata, and test logs—with SHA-256 checks. See the English evidence index and checksum manifest. This archive adds no new model validation: external MLB/Statcast raw responses were not included, so the full raw-data reconciliation cannot be rerun from the metadata archive alone.
