What ClaudeBeat Taught Me Before I Built Needlbar

November 9, 2026

I built ClaudeBeat to answer one question from the macOS menu bar. How much Claude quota do I have left before it resets? Shipping a signed, notarized app that people could install with Homebrew was a good experience. Maintaining it taught me what could go wrong with a number that looked simple.

When a gauge disappeared

At one point, Claude's usage response included limits for individual models but omitted the all-models total. ClaudeBeat dropped the total gauge even though it still had a previous reading. The fix fell back one limit at a time. A partial response could update the numbers it carried and leave the others visible.

I carried that lesson into Needlbar. Claude Code, Codex, and Cursor each refresh on their own, and a failed refresh can leave the last known quota visible. Token counts and estimated cost come from local session files, while quota and reset times are shown separately. That way, the app can still tell me what I used when it has trouble refreshing how much I have left.

One app to maintain

Keeping ClaudeBeat active would mean another app to test, sign, and release when something changes. ClaudeBeat earned its place by getting the idea out into the world. I retired it so I can spend that maintenance time on Needlbar, where I now track all three tools in the same menu bar.
